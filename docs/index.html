From Sewers to Surveillance: Monitoring Salmonella Through Wastewater.

Kuhn KG, Shelton K, Rimal B et al.. From Sewers to Surveillance: Monitoring Salmonella Through Wastewater.. Methods in molecular biology (Clifton, N.J.). 2027

Wastewater surveillance offers a population-level approach to infectious disease monitoring that can detect cases missed by clinical reporting, including asymptomatic and unreported infections. This study developed and validated methods for detecting Salmonella in wastewater, combining sample concentration through polyethylene glycol precipitation, magnetic bead-based nucleic acid extraction, and quantitative PCR. These methods were deployed across a statewide surveillance network to track Salmonella transmission over a two-year period. The surveillance system revealed seasonal transmission patterns, provided approximately one week of advance warning relative to clinical case reporting, and detected outbreak activity that passive surveillance systems failed to capture. While limitations such as low pathogen concentrations and difficulty resolving serovars remain, the findings support wastewater surveillance as a valuable early-warning and outbreak detection tool when used alongside traditional public health approaches.

Machine learning-enabled wastewater-based surveillance for emerging pathogen detection and monitoring: current applications, challenges, and future prospects.

Song A, Jia P, Luo J et al.. Machine learning-enabled wastewater-based surveillance for emerging pathogen detection and monitoring: current applications, challenges, and future prospects.. Emerging microbes & infections. 2026

This review examined how machine learning is being applied to wastewater-based surveillance to detect and monitor emerging pathogens at the community level. The authors conducted a narrative review of published literature, classifying methods into four tiers ranging from classical statistical models through deep learning and hybrid mechanistic approaches, and evaluated their application across five analytical tasks including forecasting, anomaly detection, and data harmonization. They found that the evidence base is heavily skewed toward studies of a single pathogen, with most other target-task combinations remaining prospective rather than validated, and that early-warning performance came predominantly from simpler statistical methods rather than more complex machine learning approaches. Reported predictive performance was consistently context-dependent, and the field faces substantial barriers related to data quality, sampling variability, inter-laboratory inconsistency, and population-level biases that limit generalizability. The authors conclude that future progress requires standardized collaborative frameworks, multimodal data integration, mechanistically informed modelling with uncertainty quantification, and attention to governance and public acceptability before broader surveillance applications can be realized.

This study evaluated whether air sampling in airport environments could serve as an effective surveillance tool for respiratory pathogens at points of international entry. Researchers monitored four major U.S. international airports over approximately ten months, collecting air samples from congregate areas and comparing the results against aviation wastewater, traveler nasal swabs, and national clinical surveillance data. Both SARS-CoV-2 and influenza A were frequently detected in air samples, with influenza A positivity showing strong correlations with other surveillance modalities, particularly national clinical data, while SARS-CoV-2 measurements did not correlate consistently across methods. The findings support air sampling as a complementary approach to wastewater-based surveillance at airports, though the utility varies depending on the specific pathogen and surveillance modality being compared.

Flownomaly: Single-Step Conditional-Mean Recovery for Few-Shot Anomaly Detection

Bernardo Perrone Ribeiro, Erkut Akdag, Egor Bondarev. Flownomaly: Single-Step Conditional-Mean Recovery for Few-Shot Anomaly Detection. arXiv. 2026 preprint

The paper addresses few-shot industrial anomaly detection, where models must learn what "normal" looks like from only a small number of defect-free images and then identify deviations in new queries. The authors propose Flownomaly, which frames the problem as recovering a normal counterpart for a given query in the feature space of a frozen pretrained vision model, using conditional flow matching to learn a velocity field that transports Gaussian noise toward normal patch features. A key design choice is that inference requires only a single evaluation of the learned velocity field rather than full trajectory integration, yielding the posterior mean of the clean normal token efficiently, and an operating time parameter is selected automatically using a normal-only Jacobian criterion that requires no anomalous validation samples. Anomaly scores are derived from squared residuals between query tokens and their recovered normal counterparts, enabling both pixel-level localization and image-level detection. Evaluated across multiple few-shot benchmarks, Flownomaly achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to recent category-specific methods, with particularly notable gains in the one-shot setting.

A Central Limit Theorem for the Driver Site Frequency Spectrum

Armaan Ahmed, Jasmine Foo, Kevin Leder. A Central Limit Theorem for the Driver Site Frequency Spectrum. arXiv. 2026 preprint

This paper studies the statistical properties of the driver site frequency spectrum (SFS) in a supercritical two-type continuous-time linear birth-death process that incorporates mutation and selection, where mutant individuals have a higher net-growth rate than wild-type individuals. Building on previously established law of large numbers results for the SFS, the authors derive a second-order central limit theorem by demonstrating stable convergence of a suitably scaled deviation of the SFS from its first-order limit. These theoretical results are then applied to construct confidence intervals for a class of consistent estimators of the fitness advantage conferred by the driver mutation.

If at First You Don't Succeed, Backtrack: Lightweight Recovery for Frozen VLAs

Thomas Landeg, Thierry Tambe. If at First You Don't Succeed, Backtrack: Lightweight Recovery for Frozen VLAs. arXiv. 2026 preprint

This paper addresses the brittleness of Vision-Language-Action models in robotic manipulation, specifically their tendency to fail when their own actions lead them into states underrepresented in training data. The authors introduce Backtrack, a lightweight mechanism that adds a small failure prediction head trained on outcome-labeled rollouts of a frozen policy, using the policy's existing internal representations to estimate the likelihood of episode failure without requiring recovery demonstrations, retraining, or additional models at inference time. When predicted failure probability exceeds a threshold, the system pauses the policy, physically retraces the robot arm's recent motion, and resumes the unchanged policy from an earlier configuration. The approach was evaluated across multiple VLA architectures spanning different action-generation families on robotic manipulation benchmarks, finding that it meaningfully reduces failure rates on long-horizon tasks while having a smaller effect on shorter tasks where baseline success rates are already high. The failure prediction head adds only a negligible fraction to the total parameter count, making the method broadly applicable to frozen, pretrained policies without modification.

Generating entanglement in quantum matter with pathway-entangled photons via resonant inelastic X-ray scattering

Chunjing Jia, Valentin Walther, Laimei Nie. Generating entanglement in quantum matter with pathway-entangled photons via resonant inelastic X-ray scattering. arXiv. 2026 preprint

This paper proposes a new use for resonant inelastic X-ray scattering, shifting its role from a probe of quantum materials to a tool for actively generating entanglement within them. The protocol splits an incident X-ray photon coherently across two arms of an interferometer, directing each arm to opposite ends of a solid-state sample, then recombines the scattered photons at a second beam splitter so that detecting an energy-selected photon heralds a coherent superposition of excitations at the two sample ends. This approach is framed as the X-ray solid-state analogue of the established DLCZ heralding scheme, analyzed using a Kramers-Heisenberg formalism with quantized photon modes. The authors demonstrate the mechanism across three model systems, showing that the protocol can generate heralded Bell states between decoupled sites, reveal reflection-parity-dependent interference in a tight-binding chain, and produce entangled fractionalized edge spins in a spin-1 Haldane chain. Practical challenges for experimental realization in X-ray settings are also discussed.
