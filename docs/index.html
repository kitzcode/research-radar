Mila Shakya, Kevin C. Ma, Laura J. Hughes et al.. Early Detection and Surveillance of the SARS-CoV-2 Variant BA.3.2 — Worldwide, November 2024–February 2026. MMWR Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report. 2026

The paper examined the emergence and global spread of the SARS-CoV-2 variant BA.3.2, which was first identified in South Africa in late 2024. Using a multimodal genomic surveillance strategy incorporating traveler-based screening, clinical specimen testing, and wastewater monitoring, public health authorities tracked the variant's introduction and dissemination within the United States and internationally. BA.3.2 carries a large number of substitutions and deletions in the spike protein relative to the antigens used in current COVID-19 vaccines, raising concern that it may reduce the effectiveness of vaccine-induced or infection-acquired immunity. By early 2026, the variant had been detected across multiple surveillance streams in dozens of states and reported by more than two dozen countries. The authors conclude that continued genomic surveillance is essential to monitor the variant's trajectory and assess its potential public health impact.

Metagenomic Sequencing for Wastewater-Based Surveillance: Modeling and Experimental Approaches for Determining Limit of Detection

Xiao A, Besse K, Connors D et al.. Metagenomic Sequencing for Wastewater-Based Surveillance: Modeling and Experimental Approaches for Determining Limit of Detection. 2026

This paper investigated the use of metagenomic next-generation sequencing as a method for wastewater-based surveillance of pathogens, addressing a key gap in understanding its performance characteristics compared to established PCR-based approaches. The researchers developed a computational model to estimate the limit of detection for various sequencing approaches applied to a local wastewater system, and conducted two types of experiments using a surrogate organism introduced either in a laboratory setting or directly into a real-world wastewater system. The findings demonstrated that detection performance varies depending on the specific sequencing method chosen and the bioinformatic analysis pipeline applied to the data. Importantly, the study showed that site-specific characterization is necessary when deploying metagenomic sequencing for surveillance, as differences in wastewater system configuration, background microbial communities, and sequencing inhibitors can meaningfully affect outcomes. The work advances understanding of when and how metagenomic sequencing can move beyond predefined pathogen lists to enable broader, untargeted pathogen monitoring in community wastewater.

VLASS Discovery of a Luminous Galactic Radio Transient Evolving on Decade Timescales

Jessie M. Miller, Gregg Hallinan, Dillon Dong et al.. VLASS Discovery of a Luminous Galactic Radio Transient Evolving on Decade Timescales. arXiv. 2026 preprint

This paper presents a detailed multiwavelength study of a luminous radio transient of Galactic origin discovered in the Very Large Array Sky Survey that had gone undetected in a prior survey conducted roughly two decades earlier. Radio monitoring revealed the source brightened over roughly a decade before declining and then rebrightening, while its extreme radio luminosity at a distance of at least fifteen kiloparsecs points to sustained accretion onto a compact object. Despite this radio power, the source is anomalously faint in X-rays compared to known Galactic X-ray binaries, and its radio spectra and temporal behavior are also inconsistent with that population. Very long baseline interferometry and equipartition arguments show that the radio-emitting region has expanded little or not at all over more than five years despite substantial spectral changes, and near-infrared spectroscopy reveals a broad emission line tracing a persistent, asymmetric high-velocity outflow. The authors propose that the source may be a young analog of the microquasar SS 433, in which a dense disk wind confines a continuously powered synchrotron jet, simultaneously explaining the compact radio morphology and the suppressed X-ray emission.

Event-Time Confounding Under Bursty Human Dynamics

Michael Iannelli, Alan Ai. Event-Time Confounding Under Bursty Human Dynamics. arXiv. 2026 preprint

This paper investigates a systematic methodological flaw in studies of digital behavior that measure the effect of user-initiated events—such as opening an AI assistant or clicking a recommendation—by comparing activity before and after the event. The core problem identified is that because users choose when to initiate these events, the chosen moment tends to fall within an already-ongoing burst of activity, meaning post-event activity may simply reflect the continuation of that episode rather than any causal response to the event.
