Ecological and social cues orchestrate concerted courtship in Drosophila

Philipp Brand, Katherine Keller, Rory T. Coleman et al.. Ecological and social cues orchestrate concerted courtship in Drosophila. Nature. 2026 commentary, not primary research

This study investigated how ecological and environmental factors shape mating behavior in Drosophila fruit flies, moving beyond the conventional focus on direct sensory signaling between sexual partners. Using Drosophila erecta, the researchers found that mating is strictly dependent on the combined ecological and social conditions created when flies aggregate in groups on food patches. Food volatiles directly elevate male sexual arousal, prompting mate-sampling behavior that generates visual motion across the group, which in turn further amplifies arousal in neighboring males through a multisensory feedback loop that coordinates courtship collectively. By comparing D. erecta with the related species D. melanogaster, the researchers showed that while food odor can promote arousal in both species, it is only an essential sensory gate in D. erecta, with circuit analyses revealing that species-specific differences in courtship drive arise from variation in how olfactory inputs modulate conserved neural nodes. These findings demonstrate that environmental cues unrelated to direct sexual signaling can profoundly restructure reproductive behavior and social dynamics, ensuring that mating is concentrated in contexts where the likelihood of reproductive success is high.
